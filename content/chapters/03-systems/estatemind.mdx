## TL;DR

- **Problem:** Tunisian real-estate valuation, legal research and market analysis are scattered across sources with no unified, explainable tooling.
- **What I built (as team lead, team DataNova):** An explainable ML valuation pipeline, a <Term id="retrieval-augmented-generation">legal RAG</Term> assistant over Tunisian corporate/securities/tax law, a five-site scraping pipeline, and market forecasting/simulation — behind a Django REST API and React app.
- **Outcome:** A working capstone platform with measured valuation accuracy (R² 0.37–0.53 depending on property type) and an honestly disclosed overfitting gap.

## Problem & constraints

Tunisian real-estate intelligence — pricing, legal context, market trends — is fragmented. EstateMind unifies valuation, legal Q&A, scraped market data and forecasting behind one platform, built as an ESPRIT capstone by team DataNova.

## Architecture

- **Valuation pipeline:** CLIP (`openai/clip-vit-base-patch32`) zero-shot image verification → BERT description sentiment (fine-tuned on data generated from the dataset) → per-property-type CatBoost / ExtraTrees models with a global fallback → market comparables → confidence scoring → SHAP price drivers → what-if scenarios. Training is <Term id="leakage-free-training">leakage-free</Term>: no price-derived input features, and neighbourhood price priors are fit on the training fold only, with leave-one-out self-exclusion.
- **Legal RAG assistant** over Tunisian corporate/securities/tax law (23 articles): `paraphrase-multilingual-mpnet-base-v2` embeddings over a French/Arabic corpus, ChromaDB with a cosine relevance threshold, and a generation <Term id="fallback-chain">fallback chain</Term> — a hosted OpenAI-compatible provider, then OpenRouter, then an extractive fallback built directly from retrieved passages when every LLM is unreachable.
- **Data acquisition:** scrapers for Mubawab, Tayara, Tecnocasa, Tunisie Annonce and Bigdatis on a shared retry/backoff client, feeding a dedup → wrangle → load pipeline.
- **Market analytics:** an interactive price/demand heatmap and delegation analytics, delegation/governorate price forecasting, a local rule-based chatbot, and a Mesa multi-agent market simulation.
- **Platform:** JWT auth with OTP email verification, free/pro/investor plan gating, and Stripe PaymentIntent billing with server-side metadata — after fixing an earlier plan-upgrade bypass.

## Key decisions & tradeoffs

- **Leakage-free training over a higher on-paper score.** Excluding price-derived features and self-excluding a property from its own neighbourhood price prior costs some accuracy but keeps the reported R² honest.
- **An extractive fallback for the legal assistant.** When every hosted LLM is unreachable, the system still answers from retrieved passages directly rather than failing outright.
- **Per-property-type models with a global fallback.** Appartement, Maison and Terrain each get their own model; a global CatBoost model covers whatever falls outside those types.

## Limitations & next steps

<Term id="explainability">SHAP</Term>-explained valuation is honest about its own overfitting: the training script flags every model `fit_status: overfitting` — train R² around 0.99–1.00 against test R² of 0.37–0.53, depending on property type. Ridge baselines scored negative R². RMSE/MAE are withheld until their units are confirmed. The description-sentiment model has since moved from TF-IDF to a fine-tuned BERT model, trained on data generated from the dataset — but it now shows the same pattern: low scores against the test set. Legal RAG evaluation numbers aren't published yet. The legal corpus covers 23 articles and isn't a general real-estate law corpus. The public legal endpoint has no rate limiting yet.
